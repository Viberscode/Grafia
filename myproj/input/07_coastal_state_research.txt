Coastal State University Grid Research

Coastal State University's Grid Resilience Lab, directed by Dr. Priya Raman, became the main research partner for Aurora Bay's energy transition. The lab first gained public attention for the Raman Report, its independent review of the 2019 Harbor Blackout.

In 2021 two of Raman's doctoral students, Tomas Berg and Aisha Karim, founded Gridwise Analytics to commercialise the lab's wind forecasting models. Gridwise Analytics later won the contract to build the control software for the Kestrel Battery Storage Project.

The lab also evaluated the GreenHomes Retrofit Program, measuring energy savings across 3,000 homes. Its findings showed that combining heat pumps with insulation produced the largest savings.

Dr. Raman testified before the Aurora Bay City Council in support of the Aurora Bay Clean Energy Plan, arguing that a combination of offshore wind and battery storage was the cheapest way to make the grid resilient. She warned that the Saltmarsh Gas Plant should not be retired until Kestrel had proven itself through at least one summer peak.

The lab receives funding from the National Clean Grid Fund and from Helix Turbines, which sponsors research on floating turbine performance.
