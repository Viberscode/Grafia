GreenHomes Retrofit Program

The GreenHomes Retrofit Program is the building efficiency pillar of the Aurora Bay Clean Energy Plan. It offers grants and low-interest loans to homeowners and landlords for insulation, heat pumps, and rooftop solar panels.

The program is administered by the Office of Sustainability under Marcus Oyelaran and delivered through a network of certified contractors. Community outreach is led by the Eastside Neighbourhood Trust, a non-profit run by Rosa Delgado, which focuses on low-income and renter households.

By the end of 2025 the program had retrofitted 14,000 homes and reduced average household energy use by 22 percent. An evaluation by Coastal State University found that savings were largest in older apartment buildings in the Eastside and Kestrel districts.

The Aurora Bay Taxpayers Alliance criticised the program's administrative costs. Rosa Delgado responded that the program had lowered energy bills for families who had been hit hardest by the 2019 Harbor Blackout.

The heat pumps installed under GreenHomes increase winter electricity demand, which Aurora Bay Power and Light plans to meet with the Northwind Offshore Wind Farm and the Kestrel Battery Storage Project.
