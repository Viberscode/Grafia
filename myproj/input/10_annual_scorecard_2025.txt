Aurora Bay Clean Energy Scorecard 2025

The Office of Sustainability published its fourth annual scorecard in January 2026. Director Marcus Oyelaran reported that renewable sources supplied 52 percent of Aurora Bay's electricity in 2025, up from 18 percent in 2020.

Key milestones in 2025 included first power from the Northwind Offshore Wind Farm, the Kestrel Battery Storage Project carrying the grid through the July heat wave, and the GreenHomes Retrofit Program passing 14,000 retrofitted homes.

The scorecard identified three risks. First, delays at Helix Turbines could push back the Northwind schedule and therefore the retirement of the Saltmarsh Gas Plant. Second, rising interest rates could increase the cost of future borrowing beyond the Aurora Bay Green Bond. Third, growing electricity demand from heat pumps and electric vehicles could outpace new supply.

Mayor Elena Vasquez said the city remained on track for its 2030 target of 80 percent renewable electricity. The Coastal Climate Coalition welcomed the progress but urged faster action on transport emissions. The Aurora Bay Taxpayers Alliance, through spokesperson Gordon Hale, acknowledged that electricity rates had stayed flat but continued to question the program's administrative costs.

Dr. Priya Raman of Coastal State University described Aurora Bay as a national example of how a mid-sized city could rebuild its grid after a crisis.
