The Aurora Bay Clean Energy Plan

In March 2021 the Aurora Bay City Council adopted the Aurora Bay Clean Energy Plan, a policy that commits the coastal city to generating 80 percent of its electricity from renewable sources by 2030 and reaching net-zero emissions by 2040. Mayor Elena Vasquez championed the plan after the 2019 Harbor Blackout left 60,000 households without power for three days.

The plan has three pillars. The first pillar is offshore wind, delivered through the Northwind Offshore Wind Farm developed by Tidewater Energy. The second pillar is grid storage, delivered through the Kestrel Battery Storage Project run by the municipal utility, Aurora Bay Power and Light. The third pillar is building efficiency, delivered through the GreenHomes Retrofit Program.

Funding comes from the Aurora Bay Green Bond, a 400 million dollar municipal bond issued in 2022, and from a federal grant from the National Clean Grid Fund. The city's Office of Sustainability, led by director Marcus Oyelaran, monitors progress and publishes an annual scorecard.

Critics, including the Aurora Bay Taxpayers Alliance, argued that the 2030 target was too aggressive and that electricity rates would rise. Supporters, including the Coastal Climate Coalition, argued the plan would cut long-term costs and protect the city from future blackouts.
