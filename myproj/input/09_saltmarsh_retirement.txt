Retiring the Saltmarsh Gas Plant

The Saltmarsh Gas Plant, a 450 megawatt natural gas facility built in 1978, was for decades the backbone of Aurora Bay's electricity supply. Its failure during the 2019 Harbor Blackout exposed the risks of relying on a single ageing plant.

Under the Aurora Bay Clean Energy Plan, Aurora Bay Power and Light set a retirement date of 2028. General manager Sandra Lee made the retirement conditional on two milestones: the Kestrel Battery Storage Project completing a full summer peak, and the Northwind Offshore Wind Farm reaching at least 400 megawatts of operating capacity.

The first milestone was met in July 2025, when Kestrel carried the grid through a heat wave. The second milestone depended on Helix Turbines delivering the remaining floating turbines to the Port Linden Marine Terminal.

The plant employs 85 workers. The Saltmarsh Workers Union, led by Frank Nolan, negotiated a transition agreement under which workers would be retrained for jobs at the Kestrel facility and at Tidewater Energy's maintenance base in Port Linden.

Treasurer David Chen estimated that retiring Saltmarsh would save the city 35 million dollars a year in fuel costs, which would help repay the Aurora Bay Green Bond.
