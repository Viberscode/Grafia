The Northwind Fisheries Dispute

When Tidewater Energy published the site plan for the Northwind Offshore Wind Farm in 2022, the Coastal Fishermen's Association objected. The association's president, Liam Murphy, said that the planned turbine array overlapped with scallop and cod grounds that had supported local families for generations.

The association filed a formal objection with the Aurora Bay City Council and organised a protest flotilla in Aurora Bay harbour in January 2023. The Coastal Climate Coalition, normally a supporter of the wind farm, called for a fair settlement.

Mayor Elena Vasquez asked Marcus Oyelaran, director of the Office of Sustainability, to mediate. Over four months Oyelaran brought together Liam Murphy, James Okafor of Tidewater Energy, and marine biologists from Coastal State University.

The resulting Northwind Fisheries Agreement created a 15 million dollar fisheries compensation fund financed by Tidewater Energy, required open navigation corridors between turbine rows, and established a monitoring programme for fish populations around the floating turbine foundations.

Liam Murphy later said the agreement was a model for how offshore wind developers and fishing communities could share the ocean.
